## 1. Origin of My Conviction: Computing under Real-World Resource Constraints
My perspective on computational efficiency was forged in an environment where energy availability and bandwidth limitations are visceral operational realities rather than theoretical trade-offs. Growing up and working as an engineer in Ethiopia, intermittent power stability and network degradation taught me that efficiency is the primary prerequisite of reliable software. When building backend microservices, wasteful algorithmic complexity or unoptimized polling mechanisms do not merely increase electricity bills—they precipitate cascading failures across resource-constrained edge devices. This reality instilled in me a disciplined engineering philosophy: every CPU cycle, memory allocation, and transmitted byte carries a quantifiable physical cost.

## 2. Academic Foundations & Technical Trajectory
During my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science at St. Mary’s University (GPA: 3.20/4.00, 100% English Medium of Instruction), I built a rigorous mathematical and algorithmic foundation across Operating Systems, Distributed Systems, Computer Networks, and Architecture. For my undergraduate capstone project, I designed and evaluated a high-concurrency, fault-tolerant distributed data synchronization protocol. However, optimizing application-layer microservices revealed to me that true sustainability requires holistic, cross-layer co-design—unifying low-level network protocol efficiency, hardware telemetry, dynamic workload placement, and green energy availability.
