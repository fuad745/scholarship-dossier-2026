PART II: RESEARCH PROPOSAL (1,500 Words Max)

Title: Evaluating Sino-African Digital Infrastructure Partnerships: Cloud Sovereignty, Distributed Telecoms, and Data Governance in East Africa

1. Research Background & Rationale

The Digital Silk Road (DSR), launched under the Belt and Road Initiative, has become the dominant framework for telecommunications modernization across sub-Saharan Africa. In Ethiopia and neighboring East African states, Chinese technological enterprises (notably Huawei and ZTE) have financed, engineered, and deployed nationwide 4G/5G mobile infrastructure, fiber-optic networks, and Tier-3 national data centers. Concurrently, developing nations are grappling with the imperatives of "data sovereignty"—the legal and technical ability of a state to retain control over citizen data, critical financial transactions, and sovereign cloud workloads.

While Western academic commentary frequently evaluates Sino-African technological cooperation through the narrow lens of geopolitical rivalry or surveillance concerns, there is a pronounced deficit of empirical, technical research conducted from the perspective of African engineers and policymakers. How are localized data localization laws actually implemented across Chinese-built cloud infrastructures? What are the technological transfer mechanisms and technical capacity-building outcomes for indigenous African software engineers operating these systems?

2. Key Research Questions

1. How do Sino-African digital infrastructure partnerships balance physical infrastructure deployment with indigenous data sovereignty and national governance in East Africa?

2. To what extent does the adoption of Chinese telecommunications and cloud standards foster local technical autonomy versus technological dependency among local engineering ecosystems?

3. What lessons can African policymakers synthesize from China’s own historical trajectory in building domestic tech sovereignty, cloud ecosystems, and data governance frameworks?

3. Methodology & Interdisciplinary Framework

This project will utilize a mixed-methods, interdisciplinary approach combining technical systems analysis with political economy:

• Technical Architecture Audit: Examining the architectural topology of major Sino-African infrastructure projects in Ethiopia, including data routing pathways, server virtualization protocols, and API interoperability with global standards.

• Comparative Policy Analysis: Evaluating China’s Cybersecurity Law and Data Security Law alongside the African Union's Malabo Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection and Ethiopia’s Personal Data Protection Proclamation.

• Stakeholder Fieldwork: Conducting qualitative semi-structured interviews with Chinese tech enterprise leads in Beijing, African diplomatic representatives, and Ethiopian telecommunications engineers.

4. Expected Contributions

This research will produce actionable policy recommendations for Sino-African tech governance, providing African governments with a nuanced blueprint for negotiating equitable infrastructure agreements while advancing academic literature at Peking University on South-South digital cooperation.
